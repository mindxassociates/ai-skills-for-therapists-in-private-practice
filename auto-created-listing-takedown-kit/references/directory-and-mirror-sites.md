# Directory and mirror sites — per-site playbooks

General approach for any auto-created page: (1) verify name + NPI; (2) find the site's own removal/opt-out route — footer links, contact page, help center — before emailing anyone; (3) state in every request: who you are, your NPI, the exact profile URL, that the page was created without consent, and that you want it **removed** (not corrected), unless the site forces the correction path; (4) keep the confirmation, ticket number, or sent email.

## Healthgrades — healthgrades.com

- Profile URLs look like `https://www.healthgrades.com/providers/<name>-<code>`. Unclaimed profiles show stale or missing data (education/languages "N/A") while asserting "accepting new patients."
- **Hard truth:** Healthgrades' published policy is that it does **not** remove profiles of practicing providers; full removal is reserved for retired, deceased, or no-longer-practicing clinicians. Do not promise the clinician a removal here.
- What to do anyway: submit a removal request through the Help Center Contact Us form (helpcenter.healthgrades.com), including the NPI and profile URL, stating the profile is unclaimed, auto-created without consent, and inaccurate. Record the response.
- Fallback (only with the clinician's explicit choice): claim at the provider update portal and correct the record, or ask about the consumer-side opt-out (`compareoptout@healthgrades.com`) which limits some uses. Label this claim-and-correct, never "removal."

## WebMD Care — doctor.webmd.com / WebMD directory

- Entries are often **index-only** — visible in WebMD directory search/listing pages without a clean profile URL — and can carry a wrong city and wrong specialties (e.g. a clinical social worker listed under Psychiatry/Psychology in a city they don't practice in).
- Route: WebMD Customer Care's "Updating/Correcting Physician Directory Listing or Review Concern" help article (customercare.webmd.com) → submit a request, category **"Directory Profile & Reviews for WebMD/Vitals"** where offered. Include NPI, the listing details as displayed (city, specialties), and request **removal** of the auto-created listing; if they will only correct, say so in the report.
- Pin down how the listing actually appears (a live check of the directory by NPI/name) before filing, so the request can quote the wrong facts exactly. Save the ticket/reference number.

## npiprofile.com — NPI mirror with an opt-out

- Profile: `https://npiprofile.com/npi/<NPI>` — an NPPES mirror that may add in-network claims for specific payers.
- Route: the site offers a self-serve **Opt-Out** tool linked from its Contact page (`https://npiprofile.com/contact`); `contact@npiprofile.com` is the fallback. One of the few mirrors where removal is actually self-serve — use the tool, keep the confirmation wording.

## npi.usadb.org — NPI mirror with no route

- Profile: `https://npi.usadb.org/<taxonomy>/<NPI>-<name>/` — a pure NPPES mirror.
- **No published removal or opt-out route exists.** Do not burn time hunting for one. Report its status in the inventory ("live, no removal route") and re-check it in the monthly sweep. The only lever is the underlying NPPES record itself, which you normally do **not** alter for this purpose.

## datalabs.health — sells your NPPES record as a PDF

- Publishes a downloadable PDF mirror of the full NPPES record (`.../npi_<NPI>.pdf`) as a data product.
- No opt-out tool. Email a removal request to **Support@DataLabs.Health**: identify yourself, give the NPI and the exact PDF URL, state you did not authorize publication as a data product, and ask for removal plus a confirmation reply.

## Topline — topline.com

- Auto-generated people pages ("Generated by Topline AI"), explicitly "unclaimed," scraped from LinkedIn and similar sources.
- Route: the opt-out form at `https://company.topline.com/opt-out/` (or `help@topline.com`). Submit with the exact profile URL and keep the confirmation.

## Leave alone (legitimate / managed)

Never file removals or spam reports against: the practice's own websites; directories the practice claims and manages (e.g. Psychology Today, Headway or other platforms the practice joined deliberately); state licensing-board listings; and **NPPES / CMS itself** — it is the source registry every mirror copies, and the record there is supposed to exist. If data is wrong *in NPPES*, that is a registry update through the normal NPPES process, not a takedown.

## Sites checked with no page found (typical)

Vitals, Sharecare, Wellness.com, US News doctor finder, FindATopDoc, Doctor.com, Opencare, Healthcare4PPL frequently return nothing for a given clinician. "No profile found" is a valid inventory result — record it with the date so next month's sweep starts from evidence, not vibes.
